A NOTE FROM THE NOTE-PASSER

A little message.
A different unfolding.

One link. A different story for every passkey.
Yours brings back the words you put inside.

It started with a folded piece of paper.

When I was a kid, I used to pass notes in class to my friends. A little scrap of paper, a few words, a couple of folds. Then the very serious business of getting it across the room without the teacher noticing.

Sometimes it was a joke. Sometimes it was a secret. Sometimes it was something I would never have had the courage to say out loud. The message was half the fun. The other half was knowing someone was going to unfold it.

There’s a feeling I associate with the old man in Squid Game remembering the games he played as a kid: how something so simple can take you right back. Before everything needed an account, a feed, or a reason. Sometimes you just wanted to play with your friends.

That’s the little feeling behind PakuPaku. The folded-paper games, the classroom notes, the tiny thrill of a message meant for someone. A piece of that, brought onto a screen.

Here, the paper is a link. Your passkey brings back the words you folded inside. A different passkey unfolds a different message. A small twist on an old habit.

For the things you used to write on the corner of a notebook.
