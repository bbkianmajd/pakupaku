STAGE 2 FAILURE REPORT
======================

Conclusion: 12.3% is unacceptable. Stage 2 is not complete and the product is
not ready for acceptance. The dominant measured blocker is lexical breadth;
the remaining failures also establish substantial role, grammar and surface
coverage gaps. Adding individual example phrases is not a credible route to
99.5% development coverage.

Concrete diagnosis examples:
  - "can you please help with this one?": rejection at "please". Request
    modifiers are not composable in the inverted auxiliary/subject frame.
  - "i don't know how to say it.": rejection at "to". Embedded wh-infinitive
    complements are missing despite the individual words being recognized.
  - "it'll keep you warm.": rejection at "warm". The object-plus-predicate
    verb frame is missing; a direct-object verb classification is insufficient.
  - "haven't heard from you in a while.": rejection at "while". Its spelling
    is recognized as a conjunction, but the required count-noun role is absent.
  - "01/19/2001 09:14 am": rejection at "am". Padded clock hours are supported
    without a meridiem, but not in the 12-hour-plus-am/pm production. This is
    an implementation consistency gap, not an unfamiliar word.
  - "i will.": an incomplete ending. Modal ellipsis is not available in that
    statement production, even though some elliptical questions work.

7. RANKED IMPROVEMENT PLAN AND CONCLUSION
Priority 1 - Replace incremental headword additions with a sourced lexical
  pipeline covering common noun/adjective/adverb senses, verb senses and all
  inflections. Audit high-frequency function words completely. Permit multiple
  roles for one spelling, with explicit person/number/case, countability and
  complement frames. Review ambiguous classifications instead of guessing.
  Evidence: 693 surface-miss items, including the overlapping lexical categories
  above. Do not add just the top few words: most affected notes have several
  misses and can remain blocked after the first one is fixed.
Priority 2 - Make the grammar genuinely compositional across shared noun,
  predicate, clause and utterance features. Implement the ranked 139-item
  backlog by families: discourse/ellipsis; known-word roles and frames;
  auxiliaries; noun/quantity/partitive phrases; embedded clauses; modifiers;
  and clock combinations. Reuse the same production in statements, questions,
  requests, contractions and multi-sentence documents. Test feature products,
  not only one example from each feature. Padded hours plus am/pm is a clear
  example of individually supported features failing when combined.
Priority 3 - Expand surface/utterance handling with reversible structures for
  ordinary apostrophes, quotations, parentheses, dashes, headings, addresses,
  punctuation runs, pauses and fragments where declared in scope. Keep partial
  transcript words and specialist notation visible in all-submitted results;
  prelabel scope for new collections rather than silently excluding them.
Priority 4 - Broaden typed proper-name coverage for people, organizations,
  places and calendar names. Evaluate a reversible typed mechanism if a closed
  pool remains the limiting factor. Do not pass arbitrary unknown text through
  unchanged or assign it a guessed grammatical role under alternate keys.
  This work is part of the lexical priority; its percentages are not additive.
Priority 5 - Improve failure attribution: retain all secondary blockers,
  distinguish missing word/form/role/frame from parse structure, separate
  unsupported punctuation from transcript fragments, and count UI/codec faults
  independently only when an upstream stage passed. Track newly rescued full
  sentences and complete notes per family, alongside regressions and decoys.
In parallel - Correct sampling and obtain substantially more independent
  complete notes. Three note samples and five conversations do not support a
  deployment coverage claim. Do not tune against validation/acceptance data.

Required decision: keep Stage 2 open. Reaching 99.5% on this 1,000-item
development sentence set would require at least 995 passes: 872 additional
successful complete sentences. A family is completed only after production
composer entry, exact canonical author recovery, alternate-key replay and
regression checks pass together. Then measure untouched validation and obtain
the required independent decoy review. Neither added dictionary size nor
passing the owner examples is an acceptable substitute.

Final conclusion: the low score reflects an incomplete English lexical and
grammatical model, compounded by inconsistent composition and a weak pilot
sampling design. The highest-return route is a broad, reviewed lexical model
PLUS reusable feature-aware constructions and systematic cross-feature tests.
Do not raise the score by accepting arbitrary known-word sequences or marking
unfinished phrases complete. Retain negative controls and enforce grammatical
dependencies under alternate-key mapping.
Local phrase patches and punctuation relaxations cannot resolve an 87.7%
failure rate. The benchmark must remain honest while those families are fixed.
